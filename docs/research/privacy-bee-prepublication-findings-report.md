# Privacy Bee Prepublication Findings Report

Status: Hands-on evaluation complete — historical evidence record, not an endorsement or rating
Prepared by: Choose Better Tech
Evaluation opened: 2026-07-17
Draft updated: 2026-08-17
Current access state: Unlicensed as of 2026-08-17
Publication status: Public Privacy Bee review authorized August 19, 2026

## Purpose

This report gives Privacy Bee an opportunity to review factual observations, explain unresolved product behavior, and identify errors before Choose Better Tech decides whether to authorize a public review.

It is not a published review, rating, recommendation, ranking, endorsement, or promise of coverage. Privacy Bee may correct facts or provide supporting documentation, but it does not receive editorial approval or control over any future Choose Better Tech article.

## Evaluation relationship and independence

- Privacy Bee Pro access was provided for a 30-day independent evaluation.
- The account displayed a monthly plan value of $39 and an access end or renewal date of 2026-08-17.
- No payment method or invoices were visible during the evaluation.
- No required rating, editorial approval, favorable-treatment condition, publication commitment, or affiliate activation was identified.
- Choose Better Tech did not sign the Limited Power of Attorney.
- No payment method was added and no auto-renew action was taken.

## Evidence and privacy boundaries

The evaluation used the live product, sanitized notes, private screenshots, and limited external verification. Personal identifiers, authentication material, private URLs, exposure IDs, and the confidential downloadable report are excluded from this document.

The initial exposure report states that it may not be published or redistributed without prior consent. This report summarizes product behavior but does not reproduce that report.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Observed fact:** directly seen during controlled product use.
- **Provider claim:** stated by Privacy Bee or its interface but not necessarily independently verified.
- **Inference:** cautious interpretation of observed evidence.
- **Unresolved:** current evidence does not support a conclusion.

## Executive summary

Privacy Bee presented a broad, feature-rich privacy dashboard with identity classification, broker monitoring, search-result preservation, dark-web monitoring, downloadable reporting, manual deletion guidance, and strong account-security options. The account showed substantial product-reported workflow activity through the August 16 near-expiration checkpoint. On August 17, the account became Unlicensed, protection and monitoring stopped, and substantial historical data remained visible.

The evaluation also found material evidence-quality limitations:

- overlapping dashboard and product-area metrics use definitions that are not fully explained;
- candidate discovery included false positives and mixed identity data requiring user judgment;
- a broker record labeled Closed was likely still discoverable through the broker's public preliminary search;
- the aggregate re-exposure count did not map every event to a record, although the previously Closed StateCourts record returned as Discovered/Pending Removal and demonstrated re-detection without proving a restarted removal request;
- the human support reply explained that Completed and Closed are workflow classifications that may include outcomes other than successful removal;
- actual protection scope depended on enabled categories, with 3 of 14 controls active.
- expiration changed the requested-removal display to 0 Paused while preserving completed and in-progress workflow counts;
- the unlicensed Dark Web page displayed 25 obvious placeholder records using account-style exposure and risk language, creating a material risk of misinterpretation.

These findings do not establish that Privacy Bee is ineffective. They do mean that dashboard status labels and aggregate counts should not be treated as independent proof of removal without clearer definitions or external verification.

Privacy Bee's Customer Experience Manager acknowledged the findings on August 3, stated that the team was reviewing them, and on August 4 invited the evaluator to a Zoom call with another Privacy Bee participant for deeper product review and account-specific questions. The invitation is recorded as responsive engagement, not as resolution of the underlying findings.

### August 14 and August 17, 2026 — provider follow-up response

Privacy Bee answered six written questions after two missed calls. The provider stated that:

- it scans its full people-search-site and affiliate-network set, while removal-request applicability for other brokers depends on the broker's localities and the member's available privacy rights;
- people-search removals are checked through recurring scans, while explicit broker confirmation is sought when available;
- a reappearing record creates a new removal request and is tracked as a separate re-exposure;
- a successful removal is counted after broker confirmation or after a follow-up scan no longer finds the record;
- success rates and timelines vary too widely by broker for one headline figure;
- its differentiators are broad source scope, persistent rescanning, automation at scale, and human escalation for resistant sources;
- public records cannot always be erased and reappearance is expected;
- coverage-count methodology, SOC 2 Type II information, process documentation, and a technical-team call could be provided.

The reviewed thread did not include the offered coverage figures, counting documentation, SOC 2 material, technical documentation, success-rate dataset, or timeline benchmark. The response is substantive provider evidence but not independent verification.

The statement about when a successful removal is counted does not erase the earlier support explanation that Completed and Closed can include unsuccessful or mismatched outcomes. The report treats the newer statement as likely describing the narrower Exposures Removed concept, but that mapping remains an inference because Privacy Bee did not name the metric.

## Removal progress and external verification

### Product-reported progress

On August 6, the all-time dashboard displayed:

- 35 removals requested;
- 13 completed;
- 22 in progress;
- 1 re-exposure.

The Data Brokers view separately displayed 16 exposures removed and 100 active exposures.

On August 16, the all-time dashboard displayed 51 removals requested, 19 completed, 32 in progress, and 6 re-exposures. The Data Brokers view separately displayed 18 exposures removed and 188 active exposures. The difference between the 88 additional active broker exposures and the 21 additional dashboard exposures was not explained by the interface.

### StateCourts verification

Privacy Bee labeled one StateCourts record Closed with a last scan and last action date of 2026-07-20. The product table linked to the StateCourts homepage rather than the original public profile. Privacy Bee later explained that Closed may mean Successfully Removed, Company Unresponsive, or Data Did Not Match; the visible record did not identify which subtype applied.

With explicit owner authorization, Choose Better Tech performed a StateCourts preliminary search using the minimum available name and state identifiers and accepted the site's required terms for that search. A refined query returned 61 masked candidates. One candidate matched two location markers associated with the Privacy Bee record.

Evidence classification:

- **Observed fact:** a masked candidate matching both markers was publicly discoverable in the preliminary search.
- **Inference:** the candidate was likely the same underlying record Privacy Bee labeled Closed.
- **Limitation:** the masked result could not conclusively establish identity; a full report required registration or purchase and was not opened.
- **Conclusion:** the Closed label is not an independently verified removal and can coexist with continued public discoverability under Privacy Bee's stated definitions.

On August 16, StateCourts was absent from the 16-record Closed list and appeared as Discovered/Pending Removal with August 15 scan and action dates. This supports re-detection of a resurfaced record. It does not establish that Privacy Bee submitted or completed a new removal request.

### Questions for Privacy Bee

1. Can the product expose the Closed subtype—Successfully Removed, Company Unresponsive, or Data Did Not Match—on each record?
2. Does Privacy Bee retain and recheck the original detected profile URL before assigning Successfully Removed?
3. Can Privacy Bee identify the StateCourts Closed subtype and provide non-confidential evidence supporting it?
4. Does the single re-exposure correspond to this or another broker, and did Privacy Bee automatically restart removal?
5. When a tracked broker redirects users to another people-search service, does Privacy Bee verify and remove the downstream record or close only the original-domain case?

## Identity matching and user control

### Observed strengths

- The Identity Vault preserved active and ignored records.
- False matches could be dismissed and later reinstated.
- Search Presence false positives could be preserved without permanently deleting their history.
- Broker details included captured evidence, exposed-field categories, deletion guidance, and advisories.

### Observed limitations

- Candidate discovery included false names, false phone matches, and records mixing multiple identities or locations.
- The remaining-candidate counter did not decrease one-for-one with dismissed cards.
- Some consequential controls used terse labels and limited confirmation.
- The public exposure control sometimes routed to Privacy Bee's internal broker page rather than the original exposed profile.

### Questions for Privacy Bee

1. How is the remaining-candidate count calculated?
2. What safeguards prevent mixed-identity matches from triggering incorrect removal requests?
3. Can users access the original public profile URL for independent verification?
4. Which actions receive confirmation dialogs, and are additional safeguards planned?

## Protection scope

The account remained on a configuration showing 3 of 14 controls active:

- People Search Affiliates;
- List Brokers;
- People Search Sites.

Other categories were not active, including AdTech and Marketing, Background Check, Data Enrichment, Geolocation, Healthcare and Insurance, Job Recruiting, Map Blurring, Marketing Industry Suppression, Surveillance Tech, and Work and Income Verification.

The evaluation therefore distinguishes advertised platform coverage from protection categories actually enabled for this account.

Questions for Privacy Bee:

2. How do disabled categories affect advertised broker and custom-site coverage counts?
3. Can the product show a user-specific covered-site total based only on enabled controls?

## Support responsiveness

An in-product question about the July 16-versus-17 removal-count discrepancy received an immediate automated answer. The automated response attributed the difference to six-month versus all-time ranges, but that explanation did not fit an account created during the same month.

The question was escalated to a human on July 19 at approximately 12:41 PM Pacific, and email notification was configured. The chat widget later opened to a fresh greeting and did not expose the prior conversation. A narrow connector mailbox search also returned no match, but owner-supplied inbox evidence corrected that incomplete result.

The inbox evidence confirmed:

- a July 20 notification that a new Privacy Bee Support reply was available and the conversation had been resolved;
- a three-message representative thread dated July 21;
- a later representative reply dated July 31 about removal-outcome classification;
- chat transcripts dated July 19 and July 31.

The complete human response was later supplied and reviewed. Privacy Bee stated that:

- Completed means the removal process was processed, not necessarily that removal succeeded;
- rejected requests may count as Completed;
- Closed may mean Successfully Removed, Company Unresponsive, or Data Did Not Match;
- unrelated or partially matched Closed records contribute to All-Time Removal Activity;
- Exposures Removed represents individual exposures or pieces of personal information the provider considers successfully removed;
- Closed Brokers includes all three Closed categories;
- the optional LPOA may improve removal success by approximately 10%–20%, according to the representative.

The response was clearly human, substantive, and directly responsive. The apparent response interval was approximately 16 hours 25 minutes. It resolved the conceptual meaning of the metrics but did not itemize the account records composing each total.

The support question was based on an intermediate state showing 11 dashboard completions, 14 exposures removed, and 10 Closed brokers. Two Closed cases, Pipl and Deluxe, showed rejected deletion requests because signed authorization was missing, while several other Closed cases appeared unrelated or partially matched. Privacy Bee confirmed that rejected and mismatched Closed cases can contribute to dashboard activity.

The complete contemporaneous breakdown was 6 records belonging to other people, 1 probably unrelated record, 1 mixed record, and the 2 LPOA-related rejections. None of the 10 visible Closed cases clearly demonstrated successful removal of a record containing only the evaluator's information.

Questions for Privacy Bee:

1. Can Privacy Bee provide an account-level reconciliation showing which records compose Completed, Exposures Removed, and Closed Brokers?
2. Is chat history expected to remain visible to the signed-in user?
3. What service level does Privacy Bee target for human support on Pro accounts?

## Preliminary strengths

- Broad discovery and monitoring surface.
- Useful Identity Vault and ignored-record audit trail.
- Reversible false-positive preservation.
- Broker evidence, deletion guidance, and advisories.
- Visible removal workflow and downloadable reporting.
- Two-factor authentication, session visibility, and passkey support.
- Meaningful increase in product-reported completed removals during the evaluation.

## Preliminary concerns

- Metric definitions and cross-screen reconciliation are insufficiently transparent.
- False positives and mixed identities require significant user judgment.
- Product completion labels are workflow outcomes and, by Privacy Bee's own definition, are not reliable substitutes for external absence checks.
- The StateCourts Closed record appears likely to remain publicly discoverable.
- Re-exposure itemization remains incomplete; StateCourts demonstrated re-detection, but Pending Removal did not prove a restarted removal request.
- Enabled protection scope is much narrower than the top-level platform coverage figures might imply.
- Human support was responsive and substantive, but the product terminology remains easy to misread without that explanation.
- Confidentiality restrictions limit direct evidence reuse in an independent review.
- The unlicensed Dark Web preview uses placeholder records and account-style risk language that can resemble genuine exposure findings.
- Historical report listings remain visible after expiration, but the evaluated download controls produced no observed file retrieval.

## Privacy Bee response status

The August follow-up partially addressed coverage applicability, removal verification, and the intended re-exposure workflow. It did not resolve:

1. the account-level composition and cross-screen reconciliation of Completed, Exposures Removed, Closed Brokers, and active-exposure totals;
2. the StateCourts Closed subtype or whether its Pending Removal state produced a newly submitted request;
3. the identity and workflow state of the other re-exposures;
4. the user-specific meaning of the 3-of-14 active protection scope;
5. the initial report's Free-versus-Pro discrepancy;
6. independently checkable success rates, typical timelines, or source-level coverage evidence.

Privacy Bee should avoid sending unnecessary personal data in its response. Account-specific evidence may be provided privately and summarized in sanitized form.

## Evaluation completion

The hands-on evaluation is complete. A second external verification and custom URL removal close with documented limitations because no safe, non-paywalled original profile URL or clearly verified owner exposure URL became available. The August provider response is incorporated. Additional provider materials may be assessed later, but no further account action or call is required before a separate editorial publication decision.
