## Research objective and search intent

Help a beginner or mainstream buyer decide whether software solves a real problem and, if so, how to compare fit, friction, cost, limits, privacy, reliability, and exit difficulty before paying. The distinct intent is practical, software-specific purchase evaluation—not a physical technology buying guide, a product ranking, or a vendor comparison.

The Search Console query “how to choose a tech product” is treated only as a discovery signal. The article will not broaden into laptops, phones, televisions, cameras, appliances, or other physical products.

## Scope and exclusions

In scope: VPNs, password managers, cloud storage, data-removal services, cybersecurity tools, free plans, trials, recurring billing, privacy/security claims, support, export, migration, and real-task testing.

Excluded: physical consumer technology; universal product winners; current vendor pricing or plan rankings; legal advice; claims that privacy or security is absolute; affiliate-led recommendations; hands-on product verdicts.

## Planned original framework

Name: **The Better Software Decision Framework**. It is a repeatable ten-step sequence: (1) problem fit, (2) required outcomes, (3) friction and usability, (4) true cost, (5) limits and tradeoffs, (6) privacy and security, (7) provider reliability, (8) exit difficulty, (9) real-world testing, and (10) an evidence-based decision. Its original contribution is the explicit treatment of exit difficulty and a real-task test as decision dimensions alongside fit and cost, plus a stop-buying branch when the problem is not important enough to justify ongoing complexity.

## Evaluation criteria and evidence plan

The article will define each dimension, show a beginner-friendly question or test, and state a failure case. Volatile vendor details will be separated from stable principles and checked on the vendor’s current official pages only when used as an example. No vendor will be used as evidence for a broad category claim.
