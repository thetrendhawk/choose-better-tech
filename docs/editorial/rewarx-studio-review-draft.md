# Rewarx Studio review: polished product images, but check the facts

*Published September 7, 2026. Updated September 10 with Rewarx’s response and repaired evidence links. Hands-on testing and public pricing/policy checks remain dated September 7.*

Rewarx Studio can turn a basic product photograph into a convincing visual concept. Our testing also showed why a convincing image is not automatically an accurate listing image. Bottles lost visible wear, a shirt changed presentation, and generated marketing images claimed a material composition we had never supplied.

The clearest finding came from a follow-up Product Page test. We reviewed all 22 editable product fields, replaced unsupported entries, and explicitly prohibited invented material and performance claims. Two resulting images still said “PREMIUM SOFT COTTON.” Several others turned our editing instructions into customer-facing copy.

Our recommendation is limited: consider Rewarx for supervised concept work and simple mockup experiments. For condition-sensitive resale or product pages published without close inspection, the tested results give us substantial reservations. We are not assigning a whole-product star rating because current export verification and paid-plan testing remain incomplete.

**Disclosure:** Choose Better Tech has a recorded affiliate relationship with Rewarx and received 100 complimentary evaluation credits in addition to the account's baseline 10. This evaluation includes automation-assisted browser testing and review of retained test files. No new paid subscription was purchased. Testing combined recovered August records with automation-assisted live follow-up checks in September.

## Who this is most useful for

| Your task | Our assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Explore backgrounds, lighting and visual concepts | Potentially useful, provided you compare the result with the real product |
| Put a simple graphic on a mockup | Promising in our one-design mug test; inspect the final lettering and proportions |
| Show the actual condition of a used item | Poor fit for the bottle results we observed, which changed wear and geometry |
| Describe clothing fit or fabric performance | Our images cannot validate either; use measurements and verified specifications |
| Generate a finished product page without manual checks | Not recommended on the evidence from our two Product Page trials |

For an item whose exact condition matters, keeping the original photograph and making limited edits to its existing pixels is a more suitable starting point than generating a new depiction. We did not run a named competitor benchmark, so this is workflow guidance rather than a product ranking.

## What we tested

Our August 14 evaluation recorded 11 generation runs and retained 19 output files. The inputs included a worn clear bottle, a plain white T-shirt, a box with printed text, and a simple CBT TEST graphic. A damaged-box candidate was collected but not tested.

On September 7, we completed two additional trials: an eight-image Product Page set after correcting unsupported fields, and one narrow correction to a previously generated bottle image. That brings the observed sample to 13 completed generation runs and 28 outputs. We have archived files for the 19 August outputs; the nine September outputs were inspected in the application, with their settings and findings recorded. Source thumbnails and analysis retries are not additional outputs.

This was a small, deliberately selected sample from one complimentary-access account. It is not a measurement of Rewarx's overall success rate. August credit and workflow details rely on contemporaneous notes; September testing used a signed-in cloud Chrome session. The exact web-app build and browser/OS versions were not recorded. We did not test Shopify installation, video generation, 4K generation, a paid subscription, cancellation, refunds, or ordinary customer-support response times.

Our method puts product fidelity ahead of appearance. A prettier result can still be a worse listing image if it changes what the buyer is being offered. The [test records](https://github.com/thetrendhawk/choose-better-tech/blob/39a13c48f6fdbacd0e2a6d86c370acc535adc42b/docs/research/rewarx-studio-run-log.md) document the individual procedures and limits.

## Attractive images can change the product

The automatic bottle result removed conspicuous wear and changed the silhouette. More controlled prompts retained more marks, but did not preserve the original geometry and fine detail exactly. That matters for resale: scratches and discoloration are part of the item a buyer needs to see.

The box result was more encouraging. Its prominent wording remained recognizable. However, the original image already contained malformed small print and symbols, so we cannot fairly blame every fine-detail defect on Rewarx. We did not verify barcode accuracy.

The simple mug mockup was another positive result. The CBT TEST lettering and circle-and-two-line motif remained recognizable. That is useful evidence for this particular design, not a guarantee that intricate artwork or small text will survive every mockup.

Virtual Try-On produced a plausible image of a person wearing a white shirt, but changed the garment's presentation and missed the requested full-body framing. It does not tell us how the real shirt fits. Group Shot folded the shirt and made the bottle look cleaner. We excluded the Ghost Mannequin stress test from ordinary quality scoring because its flat-lay input was unsuitable for judging the mode's intended use.

## Product Page: correcting the fields did not solve the copy problem

In August, Product Page filled its editable information fields with unverified specifications and benefits. We added prohibitions in the final instruction, but left those upstream fields intact. That was an important limitation: competing input could have contributed to the result.

The September follow-up addressed it. We reviewed all 22 editable fields and replaced unsupported entries. We retained the appropriate clothing category and limited verified copy to visible facts: a plain white T-shirt, short sleeves, a crew neck, and no visible print. Material, prices, measurements, certification, shipping and performance were unknown.

Clearing several fields to blank did not hold; their earlier values reappeared. We replaced those entries with explicit instructions to omit the unknown information. We also added a final instruction against invented claims and against printing our editing notes.

The resulting set still had substantial problems:

- The ghost-mannequin hero and the white-background shirt with a fabric inset claimed cotton content.
- A quality-detail image described durable stitching and included our instruction not to claim material composition or a manufacturing process.
- Another image printed statements about there being no verified demographic targeting or additional selling points.
- A side-view image suggested precision dimensions for reliable fit, although we had supplied no measurements.

Some images followed the basic visible description more closely. The packaging image also avoided the invented cardboard-box presentation seen in August, although it printed our qualification that packaging and sale contents were unverified. Improvement in one respect did not make the complete set ready to publish.

The saved production details exposed a specific conflict: the selected theme called for cotton-related copy, followed by our additional instruction prohibiting invented cotton claims. That supports a narrow conclusion about competing instructions in this workflow. We did not inspect the internal model or establish why it resolved the conflict that way.

The practical consequence is straightforward. Filling the fields carefully is necessary, but it was not sufficient in this trial. Inspect every final headline, label and benefit statement. The [corrected-input test record](https://github.com/thetrendhawk/choose-better-tech/blob/39a13c48f6fdbacd0e2a6d86c370acc535adc42b/docs/research/rewarx-r1-corrected-matrix-2026-09-07.md) preserves the field entries and per-image observations.

## A narrow correction was more encouraging

We also asked Rewarx to widen a controlled bottle image's body and shoulders by about 10%, while preserving the neck, height, background, color and wear. The result appeared slightly broader, and the major stains, green tint and white background remained recognizable.

That is a qualified positive result. We did not measure the exact change or verify pixel-level preservation of every scratch. The correction cost one credit, matching the displayed amount. A promotion initially obstructed the refinement screen; reloading and reopening the project cleared it.

## Export readiness remains an open question

The retained August upscale is a 2048-by-2048 PNG. The retained cutout is a 1024-by-1024 RGB JPEG with no alpha channel, despite notes recording a transparent-background selection. Both need to be understood in the context of complimentary access and visible watermarks, rather than treated as evidence of a paid-plan defect.

Our current transparent-export check remains unresolved. A separate source upload did not populate in the cloud session, and a download attempt on the saved cutout did not produce a retrievable file for inspection. A missing browser download event does not prove that Rewarx failed to export the image. We therefore cannot confirm or reject its current transparency claim from that attempt.

Before relying on the workflow, inspect an actual downloaded file for the required dimensions, format, transparency and usage rights. The [follow-up log](https://github.com/thetrendhawk/choose-better-tech/blob/39a13c48f6fdbacd0e2a6d86c370acc535adc42b/docs/testing/rewarx-follow-up-test-log-2026-09-07.md) separates completed tests from blocked checks.

## Our verdict

Rewarx demonstrated useful visual range and a successful simple mockup. The narrowly requested bottle correction was also encouraging at a coarse visual level. Those strengths are real within this sample.

The limitation is product truth. Several workflows changed sale-relevant details, and the corrected Product Page trial still generated unsupported claims. If you use Rewarx, keep the original image and verified specifications beside the result, inspect every asset, and remove any claim you cannot substantiate. For an unattended product-listing workflow, this evaluation does not give us enough confidence to recommend it.

## Rewarx’s response: September 10, 2026

On September 10, Rewarx responded to our findings. It said Product Page now costs eight credits and AI Try-On one credit, and that it had updated the displayed costs. The eight-credit Product Page amount is consistent with our September 7 deduction. We have not independently verified the revised displays or subsequent deductions, and the reply did not establish when the changes took effect.

Rewarx acknowledged that preset scene prompts can introduce unrequested product descriptions and said it was testing improvements. Our September follow-up had already replaced unsupported field entries and expressly prohibited invented material claims. The reply therefore does not resolve that result or establish that the problem is fixed.

The company described automatic lighting and clarity enhancement as intentional, with preservation boundaries to be supplied through prompts. It also said Ghost Mannequin offers selectable angles. Our controlled bottle tests included preservation instructions, and the unsuitable flat-lay Ghost Mannequin stress test remains excluded from ordinary quality scoring.

Rewarx is investigating transparent exports, but has not confirmed a bug or a fix. Its general privacy and compliance assurance did not answer our specific retention and refund questions, and complimentary-output reproduction rights remain unconfirmed. These are provider statements, not independently verified product changes. Our verdict remains unchanged.

Rewarx also reported a broken test-records link. We reproduced the problem and replaced all three evidence links with permanent commit links. Those links provide written records; the generated images remain outside the public repository.
