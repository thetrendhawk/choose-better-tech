# Choose Better Tech Project Charter

Version: 1.0
Status: Active
Owner: Choose Better Tech
Effective Date: 2026-08-26

## Purpose

Help everyday people make consequential software decisions with better information and less dependence on vendor marketing, affiliate incentives, and low-value recommendation content.

## Mission

Choose Better Tech helps everyday people make better software decisions through independent research, clear explanations, evidence-based comparisons, transparent limitations, and practical recommendations based on reader fit rather than publisher revenue.

## Vision

Become one of the most trusted independent resources for people who want a clear, evidence-based answer before choosing, buying, or replacing consumer software.

## Who We Serve

### Primary audience

Beginner and mainstream consumers who need to make a software decision but lack the time, technical expertise, or desire to independently evaluate every important claim and tradeoff.

### Secondary audience

More experienced users who value independently sourced comparisons, documented tradeoffs, and concise decision support.

## Problem We Solve

Software buyers face an information-quality problem. Vendor marketing emphasizes strengths, affiliate publishers can have incentives to favor commercially attractive products, product terms and pricing change frequently, and many recommendation pages offer conclusions without enough evidence about tradeoffs, limitations, or user fit.

Consumers therefore often have plenty of information but insufficient trustworthy decision support.

## Core Values

### Reader First

The reader's interests take precedence over CBT's commercial interests. Recommendations optimize for reader fit and evidence, not publisher revenue.

### Accuracy

Being correct matters more than sounding authoritative. Important claims are verified, uncertainty is preserved, evidence types are distinguished, and mistakes are corrected.

### Transparency

Readers should be able to understand material relationships and important limitations behind CBT's conclusions. Affiliate relationships, methodology, testing limitations, automation/AI involvement where material, and substantive corrections should be disclosed under applicable standards.

### Usefulness

Every substantive piece should improve the reader's ability to make a decision. CBT explains who a product fits, who should skip it, meaningful alternatives, and consequential tradeoffs.

### Independence

Editorial conclusions belong to the evidence. Commercial opportunity may inform business planning and resource allocation but must not determine factual conclusions, rankings, criticism, recommendation labels, or verdicts.

## Operating Principles

- Research before recommendation.
- Teach before persuading.
- Do not force a universal winner when the evidence supports a conditional answer.
- Require original contribution before publication.
- Prefer primary evidence where practical and strong independent evidence where necessary.
- Value hands-on testing while never fabricating or implying testing that did not occur.
- Preserve uncertainty instead of manufacturing confidence.
- Correct material errors rather than hiding them.
- Do not scale output beyond CBT's ability to maintain accuracy and trust.
- Commercial sustainability is a project requirement; editorial compromise is not an acceptable means of achieving it.

## Strategic Thesis

There is durable demand for independent software decision support that is more rigorous, transparent, and useful than commodity affiliate content, and consistently producing that level of work can earn enough search visibility, direct audience trust, and commercial intent to support a sustainable publication.

### Key assumptions to test

1. People want this level of software decision support.
2. CBT can produce meaningfully better decision support than commodity alternatives.
3. Search engines and/or other distribution channels can surface the work.
4. Readers will trust the work enough to take meaningful action.
5. Some reader actions can eventually be monetized.
6. Revenue can be generated without compromising editorial independence.
7. The economics can support the cost of producing and maintaining unusually rigorous content.

## Non-Goals

CBT is not trying to become:

- a content farm;
- a technology-news site;
- a general gadget publication;
- an affiliate storefront disguised as editorial content;
- a review site where every product conveniently earns a positive recommendation;
- an SEO publishing operation whose primary objective is page count;
- a replacement for specialist security research or professional enterprise procurement advice;
- a vehicle for unsupported AI-generated product judgments at scale.

## Guardrails

- No fabricated hands-on experience.
- No undisclosed material commercial relationships.
- No pay-for-positive-coverage arrangements.
- No editorial ranking based on affiliate economics.
- No major factual claim presented with greater certainty than its evidence supports.
- No new content cluster without strategic justification and required approval.
- No scale that overwhelms CBT's ability to maintain accuracy.
- Material errors are corrected and significant corrections are documented.

Binding operational standards in `operations/` remain authoritative for detailed implementation and enforcement.

## Long-Term Definition of Success

CBT succeeds if it becomes a trusted and financially sustainable source of software decision support whose audience, reputation, and economics grow without weakening its editorial standards.

Long-term success requires all four dimensions:

- **Reader value:** people find CBT useful.
- **Trust:** people believe CBT earned its conclusions.
- **Reach:** enough people discover the work for it to matter.
- **Sustainability:** the economics support maintaining and improving it.

Traffic without trust is not success. Excellent research that cannot reach or sustainably serve an audience is also not sufficient for long-term business success.

## Project and Portfolio Discipline

Once foundational capability is sufficient, further building does not count as progress unless it materially advances a defined outcome, tests an important assumption, protects a demonstrated capability, or resolves a genuine blocker.

Publishing pages, adding features, expanding content clusters, building automation, and producing research are means rather than outcomes. Work should produce reader value, measurable learning, durable capability, or progress toward an approved goal.

Prior effort does not entitle a strategy, content cluster, distribution channel, or monetization path to continued resources. A well-run experiment that disproves an assumption can still be successful if it prevents larger future waste.

## Governance

- This charter should change infrequently.
- Material changes to purpose, mission, vision, values, strategic thesis, non-goals, or guardrails require an explicit documented decision rather than silent rewriting.
- SMART goals and targets are maintained separately in `GOALS.md` and may change each goal period.
- Results should inform strategy; sunk effort does not entitle a strategy to continued resources.

## Relationship to Existing Standards

This charter summarizes the strategic foundation behind CBT's existing binding operational standards. It does not weaken or replace `operations/EDITORIAL_STANDARDS.md`, the research/evidence standards, publishing workflow, monetization independence controls, corrections policies, or quality gates.
